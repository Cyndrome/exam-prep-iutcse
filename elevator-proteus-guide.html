How the circuit works

Before drawing anything, hold the whole loop in your head. It is one signal path with a feedback leg:

buttons → priority encoder → target register → comparator → direction signals → up/down counter → current floor → back into the comparator

The comparator asks one question on every clock tick: is the target above, below, or equal to where we are? Above means count up, below means count down, equal means stop. Because the counter output feeds straight back into the comparator, the system settles by itself the moment the two match. Everything else — displays, LEDs, the chaser, the distance readout — hangs off that spine as an observer.

Signal naming convention used throughout

SignalWidthMeaning
T1, T02Target floor, latched
C1, C02Current floor, from counter
UP, DN, STOP1 eachDirection decision (after override mux)
EMG1Emergency latched, active high
RST1System reset, active low
CLK12 Hz clock, shared by counter and shift register

A bar over a pin name in Proteus (or a small circle on the pin) means active low — the chip does that job when the pin is pulled to 0, not 1. Half of all wiring mistakes in this project come from ignoring those circles.

Setup

Getting Proteus open

  1. Launch Proteus 8 Professional. The window that opens is the Home tab, not the editor.
  2. Click New Project. Give it a name and a folder you can find again.
  3. On the next page choose Create a schematic from the selected template → DEFAULT.
  4. On the PCB page choose Do not create a PCB layout.
  5. On the firmware page choose No Firmware Project. Click Next, then Finish.

You now have a schematic sheet — the blue-bordered rectangle is your drawing area. Everything must sit inside it. Scroll-wheel zooms, holding the middle button pans, and F8 zooms to fit the whole sheet.

Save now and save often with Ctrl+S. Proteus will happily crash on a bad wire.

In Component mode, click the P button at the top of the parts panel. Type the part name into Keywords, double-click the matching row, keep typing the next name, then close the dialog. Four rules before you wire

1. Power comes from terminals, not from chip pins

Proteus connects VCC and GND to 74-series parts automatically, so you will not see power pins on the symbols. You still need explicit power for the discrete parts. In Terminal mode, place a POWER terminal, double-click it, and set its String to VCC; place a GROUND terminal and leave it. Drop a few pairs around the sheet rather than dragging one wire everywhere.

2. Use wire labels instead of long wires

This is the single most important habit for a schematic this size. Draw a short stub off a pin, then with the wire selected press A (or right-click → Place Wire Label) and type a name such as T0. Any other stub anywhere on the sheet labelled T0 is now electrically the same node. Use the exact names from the signal table — capitalisation matters.

3. No input may float

An unconnected input in Proteus is neither 1 nor 0, and it poisons everything downstream. Every unused input on every chip gets tied to VCC or GND. Unused gate inputs go to GND. Unused data inputs go to GND. Unused active-low control pins go to VCC.

4. Buttons need a resistor

A Proteus BUTTON is just a switch. For an active-high button: VCC → button → node, and a 10k resistor from that node to GND. For an active-low button: GND → button → node, and a 10k resistor from the node to VCC. Change a resistor's value by double-clicking it and typing 10k or 220R.

Stage 1 of 9

Buttons and the 4-to-2 priority encoder

Four call buttons must collapse into a 2-bit floor number. If two are pressed at once the higher floor wins, which is what makes it a priority encoder. Build it from gates — it is only three expressions, and you can defend every one of them in the viva.

Y1 = I3 + I2
Y0 = I3 + (I1 · I2′)
V  = I3 + I2 + I1 + I0

  1. Place four BUTTONs in a column. Wire each one VCC → button → node, with a 10k resistor from node to GND.
  2. Label the four nodes I0, I1, I2, I3.
  3. Place one 74LS32 (call it the encoder OR chip), one 74LS08, one 74LS04.
  4. Y1: OR gate 1 inputs = I3, I2. Label output Y1.
  5. Y0: inverter 1 input = I2, output to AND gate 1 along with I1. AND output plus I3 into OR gate 2. Label output Y0.
  6. V: OR gate 3 takes I1 and I0; OR gate 4 takes that result and Y1 (since Y1 already equals I3+I2). Label the final output V.

Verify on paper before moving on: pressing I2 alone gives Y1Y0 = 10 = floor 2. Pressing I1 and I2 together gives 10 as well, because I2′ kills the I1 term.

Stage 2 of 9

Target floor register — 74LS175

The encoder output disappears the instant you release the button. The register holds it until the elevator arrives. V going high is the clock edge that captures the call.

Build the reset now, since three chips will use it. Place a BUTTON, wire GND → button → node, 10k from node to VCC, and label the node RST. Pressing it drives the line low, which is what every CLR pin wants. Because MR clears the register to 00, a reset means "target is floor 0".

Stage 3 of 9

Magnitude comparator — 74LS85

This is the brain. It is a 4-bit part and you only have 2 bits, so the top half gets grounded.

A0, A1T0, T1A side carries the target
B0, B1C0, C1B side carries the current floor
A2, A3, B2, B3GNDUnused high bits must match, not float
A>B (cascade in)GNDCascade inputs set the tie-break for a single-stage comparator

A>B (out)label UP_ATarget is above us

Getting the cascade inputs wrong is the classic 74LS85 failure: leave A=B low and the outputs never assert equality, so the elevator runs past its floor forever. On the symbol these three sit on the left edge, separate from the A and B groups.

Emergency latch and manual override

The emergency flip-flop — 74LS74

Use one half of the chip as a set-reset latch through its asynchronous pins, which is the most reliable way to catch a single button press.

Press emergency and EMG stays high until you press reset. Hang a yellow LED off EMG: EMG → 220R → LED anode, LED cathode → GND.

The override mux — 74LS157 (first one)

Three SPST switches let a technician drive the car by hand. The mux chooses whether the direction signals come from the comparator or from the switches.

Wire each SPST as VCC → switch → node → 10k → GND, and label the nodes OVR, M_UP, M_DN. You also need M_STOP = NOT(M_UP OR M_DN): one OR gate and one inverter.

Stage 5 of 9

Synchronous up/down counter — 74LS191

The 74LS191 is the right choice because it has one clock and a direction pin, unlike the 74LS193 which needs two separate clocks.

First, the enable. The counter must freeze when it has arrived or when the emergency latch is set. Its enable pin CTEN is active low, so a single OR gate does the job: CTEN = STOP + EMG.

CLKCLKShared 2 Hz clock

MAX/MIN, RCOleave unconnectedOnly needed for cascading

The clock

Switch to Generator mode, choose DCLOCK, and place it on the sheet. Double-click it, set Frequency to 2 Hz, and set its name to CLK. Two hertz is slow enough to watch the floors change but fast enough not to test your patience.

The counter can reach 4–15 in principle. It never does here, because the comparator stops it the moment it equals a target that is always 0–3 and it always starts from 0. If you want belt and braces, AND the QC and QD outputs into the CTEN OR gate.

Stage 6 of 9

Seven-segment floor display — 74LS47

The 74LS47 has active-low outputs, which is exactly why it pairs with a common-anode display and not a common-cathode one.

  1. Place a 74LS47 and a 7SEG-COM-ANODE next to each other.
  2. Inputs: A → C0, B → C1, C → GND, D → GND.
  3. Control pins LT, RBI and BI/RBO all go to VCC. Leaving these floating is why a display sometimes shows nothing at all.
  4. Place seven RES at 220R between decoder outputs a–g and the display pins a–g. Match them one for one.
  5. The display's common pin goes to VCC.

Tedious but worth it: select all seven resistors, double-click one, and Proteus lets you edit them as a group. Or place a RESPACK-8 resistor array instead of seven discretes.

Stage 7 of 9

Floor arrival indicators — 74LS139 as a demux

A 2-to-4 decoder with an enable pin is a 1-to-4 demultiplexer: the enable is the data input and the address selects which output it appears on. Here the data is "we have stopped" and the address is the current floor, so exactly one arrival lamp lights, and only when the car is at rest.

The outputs are active low, so the LEDs are wired the other way round from usual: VCC → 220R → LED anode, LED cathode → the Y output. The chip sinks the current. Draw all four identically and they will light one at a time.

Stage 8 of 9

Distance display — 74LS157 + 74LS83

An adder subtracts by adding the two's complement: X − Z = X + Z′ + 1. The catch is that the answer is negative when the car is travelling down, and a negative binary number on a seven-segment display is nonsense. The fix is to feed the bigger number in first, which is what the second mux is for.

The operand swap — 74LS157 (second one)

Select pin → DN, enable pin → GND. Going up, the target is larger and goes into X. Going down, the current floor is larger and goes into X. Either way X ≥ Z, so the result is a positive distance.

Wire the second 74LS47 and display exactly as in stage 6, with its C and D inputs to GND. It now reads the number of floors still to travel, counting down to 0 on arrival.

Stage 9 of 9

Motion chaser — 74LS194

The 74LS194 shifts left, shifts right, loads, or holds depending on two mode pins. Map them straight onto the direction signals and the LED pattern walks in the direction the car is moving, and freezes when it stops.

S1S0ModeWhen
00HoldIdle or emergency
01Shift rightGoing down
10Shift leftGoing up
11Parallel loadReset — seeds the pattern

Two gating expressions get you there. Take NOT EMG from a spare inverter and NOT RST from another (call it RST_H, reset active high):

S1 = (UP · EMG′) + RST_H
S0 = (DN · EMG′) + RST_H

PinConnect to
CLKCLK
S1, S0the two gated signals above
MR / CLRVCC (reset is handled by the load instead)
A, B, C, DVCC, GND, GND, GND — the seed pattern 1000
SL (shift-left serial in)QA
SR (shift-right serial in)QD
QA … QDfour LEDs, each through 220R to GND

Feeding QA and QD back in turns the register into a ring, so the single lit LED circles round instead of vanishing after four clocks. Hold the reset button for a second at power-up to load the seed — without it the register starts at 0000 and nothing ever lights.

Simpler variant if the feedback confuses you: tie SL and SR to VCC and MR to RST. The LEDs then fill up like a progress bar while moving and clear on reset. It satisfies the same requirement.

Test plan

Press the blue play button at the bottom left to simulate. Pins turn red for logic 1, blue for logic 0, and grey for floating. Run these in order — each one isolates a different block, so the first failure tells you where to look.

  • Hold reset for one second. Display reads 0, distance reads 0, the floor-0 arrival LED lights, one chaser LED is on.
  • Press the floor 3 button once and release. The display counts 0, 1, 2, 3 at half-second intervals, the green LED is on throughout, the distance display counts 3, 2, 1, 0, the chaser walks one way.
  • On arrival: green LED off, counting stops, floor-3 arrival LED lights, chaser freezes.
  • Press floor 1. The car counts down, red LED on, chaser walks the other way.
  • Press floor 2 and floor 3 together. The car goes to 3 — priority works.
  • Mid-travel, press emergency. Everything freezes and the yellow LED latches on. Pressing floor buttons does nothing. Reset clears it.
  • Flip the override switch, then flip manual up. The car moves with no target set. Flip both off and it stops.

To watch a signal you cannot see, switch to Debug mode and drop a LOGICPROBE onto any wire. It shows the live value during simulation.

When it doesn't work

SymptomCause
Grey pins everywhereA floating input upstream. Trace back to the first grey pin and tie it off.
Nothing moves at allNo clock placed, or the DCLOCK is not actually touching the CLK net, or CTEN is stuck high.
Car never stops at the target74LS85 cascade A=B input is not at VCC, or C0/C1 feedback is reversed.
Car runs the wrong wayD/U polarity: high means down on the 74LS191. Swap UP and DN if your part behaves differently — check the symbol for the bar over U or D.
Display is blankLT / RBI / BI pins not at VCC, or a common-cathode display was placed instead of common anode.
Display shows a garbled digitSegment wires crossed. Check a–g are in order on both chips.
Target resets every clockThe 74LS175 clock is wired to CLK instead of V.
Two wires look joined but aren'tCrossing wires only connect where a solid dot appears. Delete and redraw the junction.
Simulation refuses to startRead the Simulation Log tab at the bottom — it names the offending part.

Viva questions worth preparing

  • Why a priority encoder and not a plain encoder? Two simultaneous presses on a plain encoder produce a garbage OR of both codes — pressing floors 1 and 2 would output 3, a floor nobody asked for.
  • Why synchronous and not ripple? All flip-flops in the 74LS191 change on the same edge, so the comparator never sees a transient intermediate count and cannot issue a false stop.
  • Why is the emergency stop asynchronous? Safety inputs must not wait for a clock edge. The PRE pin acts immediately, independent of CLK.
  • Where is the feedback loop? Counter output → comparator input → counter direction and enable. It is a closed-loop control system, which is why it settles rather than oscillating.
  • What limits it to four floors? The 2-bit bus. Eight floors needs 3 bits: an 8-to-3 encoder, three comparator bit pairs, a 3-to-8 decoder, and two display digits.

Build it stage by stage and simulate after each one. Nine small working circuits are far easier to debug than one large broken one.
