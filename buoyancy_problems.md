# Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle — Practice Problems
### High School Freshman Level (Moderate → Hard)

**Useful constants:** density of water = 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³), g = 9.8 m/s² (or 10 m/s² for simpler numbers)

---

### 1. (Moderate) Simple Float or Sink
A block of wood has a density of 600 kg/m³. Will it float or sink in water? Explain using the concept of density comparison, then calculate what fraction of the block's volume will be submerged if it floats.

---

### 2. (Moderate) Buoyant Force from Displacement
A stone with a volume of 0.002 m³ is fully submerged in water. Calculate the buoyant force acting on the stone.

---

### 3. (Moderate) Apparent Weight Loss
A metal ball weighs 5.0 N in air. When fully submerged in water, it weighs 3.5 N on a spring scale.
(a) What is the buoyant force on the ball?
(b) What is the volume of the ball?

---

### 4. (Moderate–Hard) Specific Gravity Calculation
An object has a mass of 250 g and a volume of 100 cm³.
(a) Find the object's density.
(b) Find its specific gravity.
(c) Predict whether it will float or sink in water, and justify your answer.

---

### 5. (Moderate–Hard) Floating Fraction
An iceberg has a density of 920 kg/m³ and floats in seawater with a density of 1025 kg/m³. What percentage of the iceberg's volume is above the water's surface?

---

### 6. (Hard) Finding Unknown Density from Floating Depth
A rectangular wooden block measuring 20 cm × 10 cm × 8 cm floats in water with 6 cm of its height submerged. Calculate the density of the wood.

---

### 7. (Hard) Two-Liquid Comparison
A ball floats in water with 30% of its volume above the surface. The same ball is then placed in oil (density = 850 kg/m³) and floats with 10% of its volume above the surface.
(a) Find the density of the ball using the water data.
(b) Verify your answer is consistent using the oil data (within reasonable rounding).

---

### 8. (Hard) Combined Object (Two Materials)
A solid sphere is made of two materials: an inner core of lead (density 11,340 kg/m³) with volume 40 cm³, and an outer shell of cork (density 240 kg/m³) with volume 260 cm³.
(a) Find the total mass and total volume of the sphere.
(b) Find the average (overall) density of the sphere.
(c) Will the sphere float or sink in water? If it floats, what fraction is submerged?

---

### 9. (Hard) Crown Problem (Archimedes-Style)
A crown weighs 7.84 N in air. When submerged in water, it weighs 7.00 N.
(a) Calculate the volume of the crown.
(b) Calculate the density of the crown material.
(c) If pure gold has a density of 19,300 kg/m³, is the crown pure gold? Explain what your result suggests.

---

### 10. (Hard) Floating Boat with Added Cargo
A rectangular barge measures 4 m long, 2 m wide, and floats in water with 0.5 m of its height submerged when empty.
(a) Calculate the mass of the empty barge.
(b) If 3000 kg of cargo is added, calculate the new depth of submersion (assuming the barge doesn't tip and stays rectangular).
(c) The barge can safely submerge up to 1.2 m before water spills over its sides. What is the maximum additional cargo mass it can carry (in total, beyond the empty barge's own weight)?

---

6. ρ_wood = 750 kg/m³
